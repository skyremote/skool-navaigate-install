Listen to this lesson in my voice

You can spot it in a second. The em dash, then another. The word leverage doing work no human would ask it to do. Every paragraph the same height. That texture has a name now — slop — and the reason your AI produces it is simple: a language model wrote it, and left to its own devices a language model writes like a language model.
The moment a reader clocks that smell, the thinking underneath does not matter. A strategy memo that reads like a chatbot gets skimmed and binned. If you are going to put the OS in charge of any prose a human will see — and you are — you fix the writing before it leaves the building.

What it enforces

Twelve core rules: no em dashes, vary the sentence length, start with the content, kill the binary contrasts, have an opinion. A banned-word list — leverage, delve, robust, seamless, transformative, and the rest of the fingerprint. And the structural tells: hedging, dodging a plain "is", three caveats on a point that needed none, the model narrating what it is about to do.
The self-check is the point. It drafts, it grades its own draft, it rewrites the failures before you see them.
